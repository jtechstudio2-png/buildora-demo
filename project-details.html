Harbourline Business Park.

Three Grade-A office buildings, a shared two-level basement, and a district cooling plant — delivered in sequential handovers on a constrained site between two occupied towers.

Project overview

Harbourline Business Park comprises three office buildings of G+8, G+10, and G+12 arranged around a landscaped plaza, with 64,000 m² GFA, a 1,100-space two-level basement, and an on-site district cooling plant serving all three buildings. Buildora was appointed main contractor under a FIDIC Red Book (1999) lump-sum contract, with contractor's design responsibility for temporary works and the façade.

The employer required Building A to be handed over in month 18 to meet an anchor tenant's lease commencement, with Buildings B and C following at months 23 and 26 — all while two neighbouring towers remained fully occupied.

The challenge

  • Excavation to −9.5 m adjacent to occupied buildings, with a high water table at −2.1 m
  • Single site access shared with neighbouring tenants' service traffic
  • Two summers of construction under midday work restrictions
  • A shared central plant that had to be commissioned before the first handover
  • GSAS Design & Build 4-star target, requiring documented material and waste performance

Our solution

We designed a secant-pile shoring wall with two levels of temporary steel props and a closed-loop dewatering system with continuous settlement monitoring of the neighbouring structures — movement stayed within 6 mm against a 15 mm trigger level. The basement was split into three pour zones so Building A's core could start while excavation continued under B and C.

Night-time delivery windows, a just-in-time concrete schedule with two batching plants, and off-site prefabricated MEP risers reduced on-site labour by 18% at peak. A federated BIM model coordinated to LOD 400 resolved 2,340 clashes before installation, and the district cooling plant was commissioned in month 16, two months ahead of the first handover.
